Road Safety Investigation Priorities in Great Britain

Analysed 513,801 police-recorded injury collisions in Great Britain to identify when estimated fatal or serious collisions concentrate and how severity composition varies by posted speed limit.

My contribution and analytical decisions

I designed and built this Tableau project for my MSc in Artificial Intelligence at UWE Bristol. From my work in intelligent transport systems, I brought a practical question to the analysis: if a road-safety team can investigate only a few patterns in detail, which findings should guide its first review?

I defined the unit before comparing the results

I chose a collision-level analysis and reconciled 513,801 records against 513,801 distinct collision IDs. I used the Department for Transport's adjusted-serious values to make comparisons across years with different reporting practices, and kept the fractional contributions until aggregation. Checking the field types changed the 2025 adjusted-serious total from about 24,657 to 25,743. That experience taught me to reconcile calculations before trusting a convincing chart.

I checked whether the headline held up year by year

I built a weekday–hour heatmap to reveal concentrations and used a separate ranking for exact comparisons. Friday at 15:00 led the pooled result, so I ranked periods separately within each year. Friday at 15:00 led in three years. Every annual peak fell on a weekday between 15:00 and 17:59. I revised the headline to reflect the pattern that held across all five years.

I chose measures around the planning decision

I displayed counts and severity shares separately because they answer different questions. The greater recorded burden on 30 mph roads supports one line of enquiry, while the higher severity share on rural 60 mph roads supports another. To estimate risk per journey or recommend an intervention, I would first add traffic exposure and local road evidence.

When and where should teams investigate?

Road-safety teams can use this national analysis to select topics for local assessment. Collision counts describe the recorded burden, while within-category shares describe the severity composition of those collisions.

Across years, weekday afternoons were the consistent signal

Friday 15:00–15:59 led the five-year total at approximately 1,787 estimated fatal or serious collisions. Friday 16:00–16:59 followed at approximately 1,767. The gap was about 20 over five years. Annual rankings placed Friday at 15:00 first in three years, with a Thursday and a Wednesday leading the other two.

From STATS19 records to Tableau measures

Each row represents one collision. The 513,801 rows reconciled to 513,801 distinct collision IDs. The records cover personal-injury collisions on public roads reported to police in Great Britain. The reporting system changed during the study period. The share of records using injury-based reporting rose from about 49.6% in 2021 to 86.5% in 2025.

Measure definitions

  1. Give each fatal collision a contribution of one. For every other collision, use the DfT-supplied adjusted-serious value.
  2. Sum the contributions at the desired level, preserving fractional values until display.
  3. For a severity share, divide that sum by the distinct number of recorded collisions within the same category and selected area.

Changing the Tableau adjustment fields from integer to decimal increased the 2025 adjusted-serious total from approximately 24,657 to 25,743. This check concerns the adjusted-serious measure specifically. The correction preserved fractional contributions that integer conversion truncated.

Visual design and checks

The Tableau dashboard pairs a weekday–hour heatmap with zero-based bars for severity share by posted limit. A shared area filter controls both dashboard views. A fixed scale of 0–1,800 estimated collisions supports comparisons between national and rural heatmaps. A separate ranking supports precise comparisons by hour. Ranking within each year tests whether the pooled result persists. The three records with missing speed limits remain in the source and are excluded from the posted-limit bar chart.

Use the findings to scope local assessments

  • Recorded burden. Review 30 mph settings when the objective is to understand where the largest volume of severe collisions was recorded.
  • Severity composition. Investigate rural 60 mph settings to understand which local road and traffic conditions accompany their comparatively high severity share.
  • Timing. Check whether the national weekday-afternoon pattern also appears in local records, with traffic volumes and journey exposure as context.

The analysis is descriptive. Estimation, under-reporting and differences in road conditions limit interpretation. Traffic volume and distance travelled would be needed to estimate risk per journey. Posted limits describe the road category. Vehicle speeds, junction design and lighting require local evidence. The combination of rural 60 mph roads and weekday afternoons remains untested.
